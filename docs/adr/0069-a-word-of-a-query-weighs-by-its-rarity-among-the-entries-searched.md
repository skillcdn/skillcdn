# ADR-0069: A word of a query weighs by its rarity among the entries searched

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-23
- Builds on [ADR-0063](0063-packs-of-documents-are-served-to-agents-through-structure-words-and-meaning.md) (point 4, the word rules, and the ranking by `ts_rank` mirrored in memory).

## Context

The rank of an entry for a query has been what `ts_rank` computes with the field weights and the length normalization, and the same computed in memory for the command: every lexeme of the query counts the same, and what tells the entries apart is how often, and in which field, each occurs. The second evaluation set showed what that does on a pack full of codes and designations. A question that quotes a code verbatim is answered first for fewer than half the questions although every code occurs in exactly one document, because the prefix every document of the domain repeats outweighs the number next to it, and the name of an institute and the word for a form outweigh the number of the form. The languages read without a stemmer or stop words lose the same way: the function words of a question match in every document and lift the maps and the long documents above the answer. The scripts matched by pairs of characters name the wrong section first for half the questions, because the common pairs match everywhere.

`ts_rank` knows nothing of rarity. The usual remedy, weighing each word by the inverse of how many documents hold it, is what every ranking function since tf-idf does; BM25 smooths it so that a word in every document weighs next to nothing without going negative.

Weighing alone moved the codes little, and the reason was the parser: it cuts a hyphenated code into its parts and a signed number, `OIW-PV-120` into `oiw`, `pv` and `-120`, as the server's parser does, and the parts are common where the code is unique, so no word of the query named the one document. The word rules have been the server's parser's, from the time the server built the vectors; since the indexer builds them (version 11), the rules are ours to set, and a vector is stored as it is built.

## Decision

1. **Each lexeme of a query weighs by its rarity among the findable entries under the searched path.** The weight is `ln(1 + (N - n + 0.5) / (n + 0.5))`, N the entries and n those holding the lexeme, as BM25 has it; the rank of an entry is the weighed mean of what `ts_rank` computes for each lexeme alone, and the sections of a document are ranked with the same weights. Counting and weighing happen at query time: nothing of the stored index changes, the reading rules keep their version, and no commit is rebuilt.
2. **The index store counts and the reader weighs.** The `IndexStore` port gains `frequencies(path, lexemes)`, the entries under the path and how many hold each lexeme, which the database answers in one pass over the mount's entries and the memory index by counting its vectors; `search` takes the weights; `queryWeights` in `core` turns the counts into weights, so both indexes weigh the same way. A store that does not count leaves `frequencies` out, and every word weighs one, which is the rank as it was.
3. **Parity holds by construction.** `ts_rank` averages over the lexemes of a query and divides by the logarithm of the length once, so the weighed mean of `ts_rank` over each lexeme alone is the rank of the weighed query, and `rankSearchVector` computes the same sum in memory. The parity test holds the two against each other with weights as without.
4. **No stop list of a further language is added for the ranking.** A word in every entry weighs next to nothing under the weight, which is what a stop list would do at query time, with no list to keep per language and no change to the index. A language gains stop words when it gains a stemmer, on the index side and the query side at once, as English has them.
6. **A match in a name counts twice a match in the body, no more.** The field weights `ts_rank` is given, and `rankSearchVector` with it, go from 1.0, 0.6, 0.2 and 0.05 for the name, the description, the path and the body to 1.0, 0.85, 0.7 and 0.5. With the steep weights a common word in the title of a short map outweighed a code in the body of a long document twenty to one, more than any rarity could make up; measured over both sets with seven settings, from the steep ones to equal weights, these reached the most first documents on both, and a skill's name still comes before a body that mentions it.
5. **A hyphenated code is a word of the vector beside its parts.** Parts of letters and digits joined by single hyphens, with a digit part or across scripts, `OIW-PV-120`, `6635-81-302-5518`, `서돌-4`, make one word as written, lowercased, and their parts follow as before; a sign (`-5`), a doubled hyphen and a version with a point are not codes. The reading rules move to version 12 for it, every index is rebuilt as it is next asked for, and a pack is served from its old index meanwhile ([ADR-0068](0068-a-mount-serves-the-last-indexed-commit-while-the-newest-is-not-ready.md)); the floor of the servable versions stays at 11, since the entries of both versions read the same.

## Consequences

- A code, a number or a name that few entries hold decides the order when a question quotes it; a word every entry repeats decides next to nothing, and still ranks the entries that hold only it above those that hold nothing of the query.
- The word rules part from the server's parser for the first time, by one addition; the parity test says so, and the English stemmer and stop words stay the server's dictionary's.
- On the two evaluation sets the words alone go from 40% to 58% of the questions answered by the first document on the library in six languages, the codes asked verbatim from 42% to 86%, and from 81% to 95% on the handbook; the numbers and what remains are in [the evaluation sets' page](../../apps/server/fixtures/retrieval/README.md). The models of phase B were measured over the words of version 11 and are not measured again here; the fusion with meaning inherits the better words.
- A search costs one more statement on the database, a count over the entries of the mount, bounded as a mount is; the memory index counts in a pass over the vectors it holds.
- The evaluation sets measure the change ([what the words reach](../../apps/server/fixtures/retrieval/README.md)), and their floors move with it.
- Rejected: weights in the stored vector (a change of the reading rules and a rebuild of every index, for a quantity that depends on the other documents of the mount); a stop list per language applied to queries alone (a list to keep per language, for less than the weight does, and an index that keeps the words its queries drop); a BM25 of our own in SQL (an extension would be a dependency against the contract of PostgreSQL alone, and `ts_rank` per lexeme is the same quantity).
