# ADR-0094: A skill is embedded as its name and description as well as its sections

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-13
- Builds on [ADR-0064](0064-meaning-search-runs-on-pgvector-and-pg-boss-behind-an-embedder-port.md) (the vectors of sections, keyed by the body), [ADR-0092](0092-a-search-across-the-packs-picks-the-parts-nearest-in-meaning-and-compares-their-sections.md) (the parts a search across the packs picks from) and [ADR-0093](0093-a-section-whose-text-has-a-vector-is-given-it-and-not-embedded-again.md) (a vector found again by its text).

## Context

A skill is chosen by two things: its name and its description. They are what a client is told when it connects, what the words of a search weigh most, and what an author writes for exactly this purpose: to say when the skill is the one to use. They stand in the front-matter of `SKILL.md`, and front-matter belongs to no section, so the search by meaning never read them. What it read of a skill was the sections of its body, each under the skill's name: the steps, the checks, the formats. A question about what a skill is for ("make a short ad from a product link") is near to what the description says and far from how the steps are worded, and the skill was found by meaning only where one of its sections happened to say what it does.

Measured on three packs read as a deployment reads them (395 findable entries, 29 of them skills, 2,585 sections, embedded with the model of the compose file) and 52 questions whose answer is known, 28 that ask for a skill and 24 for a document. By meaning alone, the skill asked for stood first for 6 of the 28 questions and among the first three for 19. With the name and description embedded as a text of their own it stood first for 12 and among the first three for 25, and the documents were found as before (first for 11 of the 24 either way). Where to keep that vector among the parts a search across the packs picks from was measured on the same vectors: as a part of its own, the skill asked for was among the parts picked for every question at every number of parts tried (10, 20 and 50 of 538), and the nearest part was the right skill's for 20 questions; in the mean of the first part, two answers were not picked at 10 parts and the nearest part was the right skill's for 16.

## Decision

1. **A skill has a head: its name and description as one text, `name: description`, embedded as a text of its own.** The vector is kept among the vectors of the skill's body, under the section number `-1`, before every section; it names no passage, so its heading path is empty and its length 0. A skill without a name has none, and nothing but a skill has one: a document's title already stands at the top of the text of each of its sections, and what the index calls its description is the opening of its body, which a section holds.
2. **One rule says what a body is embedded as** (`embeddedTextsOf` of `@skillcdn/core`): the head first where the entry is a skill, then each section as before. The worker, the `skillcdn` command and the evaluation all embed what it lists, so that a deployment and a directory find the same things by the same query.
3. **A search by meaning compares the head as it compares a section, for a skill and for nothing else.** Inside a pack and across the packs, a skill's rank by meaning is that of the nearest of its head and its sections. A body that a skill holds in one place and a document in another has the head for the skill alone: a document's result names the sections that matched, and a head is none.
4. **Across the packs, the head is a part of its own** (`-1`), beside the parts of eight sections, and not one ninth of the mean of the first part: what is picked first is compared as it stands.
5. **The rules that cut a body into sections keep their version.** Nothing about a section changed, and a new version would embed every section of every pack again for one short text a skill. The skills embedded before are given their head where they are: the worker's hourly sweep names the findable skills whose sections have vectors under the key and whose head has none, and embeds their heads, a batch at a time, for at most five minutes a sweep and under the signal of its job. Only what a slice embedded is given a head, so whether a repository is embedded at all stays the slice's to decide; the sweep at start embeds nothing, since a worker that starts should not wait on a model. The command does the same for a directory: a body whose vectors are kept without the head has the head embedded on the next run, and keeps the rest.
6. **A head written by a slice is counted as its sections are**, in the tokens and the sections a slice reports. The heads the sweep gives to what was embedded before are the deployment's own upkeep: no usage event is recorded for them and no bound consulted, a few dozen tokens a skill, once.

## Consequences

- A skill is found by meaning through what it says it does, in a pack and across packs, whatever its steps say.
- After the update that brings this, a deployment has the heads of its skills within a few sweeps: one short text a skill and nothing else embedded again. Until a skill has its head it is found as before.
- A full embedding under a new model, which is expected from time to time, writes the heads with the sections, and the sweep then has nothing to give. A head is found again by its text like any section (ADR-0093), so the same skill in a fork, or kept across a push that changed its body and not its front-matter, costs no call.
- The count of sections a snapshot's embedding reports includes the heads of its skills.
- Vectors computed elsewhere may be brought in with a head: the import takes `-1` as a section.
- A cache file of the command marks a head (`head: true`) and numbers the sections without it, so a file written before reads as it did; an older version of the command reads past the mark and takes the head for a section without a passage, which a skill's result does not show.
- Rejected: writing the description into the text of every section of a skill (every section would say the same thing first, a skill of twenty sections would cost its description twenty times, and every skill would be embedded again). Rejected: a new version of the sectioning rules (see point 5). Rejected: a table of their own for the heads (every search, purge, key change and import would read two tables for what is one more row a skill). Rejected: the head in the mean of the first part (the mean of a description and eight sections of steps is nearer to the steps).
- Not decided here: whether the order of a search across the packs should take the order of the parts into account, and how much a skill is boosted over a document there. Both are measured on a deployment's own packs first.
