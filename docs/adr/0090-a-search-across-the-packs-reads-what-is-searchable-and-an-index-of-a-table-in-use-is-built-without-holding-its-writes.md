# ADR-0090: A search across the packs reads what is searchable, and an index of a table in use is built without holding its writes

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-12
- Builds on [ADR-0070](0070-a-deployment-is-searched-across-every-pack-it-serves.md) (the search across packs, its three statements and their time limit; point 5 stands), [ADR-0069](0069-a-word-of-a-query-weighs-by-its-rarity-among-the-entries-searched.md) (the count that weighs the words) and [ADR-0079](0079-a-query-of-identifiers-alone-is-ranked-by-its-words.md) (a query of identifiers alone).

## Context

A search across packs runs three statements over the snapshots of every public repository a deployment serves: the count that weighs the words of the query, the match by words, and the comparison by meaning. What a deployment holds is mostly files. A repository that is no pack has hundreds or thousands of entries of which none or a few are searchable, and every repository somebody opens is indexed, so the entries grow with the visits while what there is to search hardly does.

The statements did not tell the two apart. The count and the comparison asked for the entries of the snapshots that are visible and have a search vector, and no index held that condition: both read every entry of every snapshot searched. The count then tested the vector of each searchable entry once for each word of the query. A search vector is kept apart from its row and unpacked for every test, so the count cost the size of all the vectors whatever the word, the same for a word nothing holds. On a database that holds its tables in memory that is a tenth of a second. On a small one that reads them from its storage the count ran out of its time, and since the count comes first, a query with a word in it was answered `search.timeout`.

Measured on a database filled to that shape (`eval:hub`: 700 repositories, 645 thousand entries, 20 thousand of them searchable in the snapshots searched, ten section vectors of 1,024 dimensions for each searchable entry of a pack), the count of a two-word query read every entry and 22 thousand pages, 159 thousand counting each time it came back to one, and the count of a word that is nowhere 87 thousand.

The migrations run in one transaction. An index cannot be built `CONCURRENTLY` in one, and built the plain way it holds off every write to its table until it is done; the index of a commit is written in one transaction, so on a deployment in use the plain build waits for those and they wait for it. The rules of the database package asked for concurrent builds, and no migration had needed one.

## Decision

1. **The searchable entries have an index of their own.** `index_entries_findable_idx` holds the snapshot of each entry that is visible and has a search vector, and nothing of the others. The total of the count and the entries the comparison starts from are read through it, and so is every count and listing of the findable entries inside a mount, whose condition is written as the index states it.
2. **A word is looked up in the index of the vectors, and what it finds is then narrowed to the snapshots searched.** The count of the entries that hold a word and the match by words both begin there. The planner is not left to choose the order: where it expects a word in many entries it reads the entries and tests each vector, which is the cost described above. So the word reaches the comparison as a value the planner does not look into, and the entries found are handed on as a whole. The counts are exact, as before: nothing in the ranking changes, and a query answers with the same results in the same order.
3. **The words and the meaning are asked at once.** An answer takes as long as the slower of the two. A query of identifiers alone is ranked by its words (ADR-0079), so its meaning is asked for only once the words have found nothing. A search holds up to two connections while both run.
4. **A statement that runs out of its time is named in the log**, at warning: `count`, `words` or `meaning`, with the limit and how many packs and words it was asked.
5. **An index of a table that is large on a deployment is built `CONCURRENTLY`, ahead of its migration.** Such a migration starts with the line `-- built: concurrently` and holds `CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS` statements alone. On a database that was migrated before, `migrate` builds each of its indexes concurrently before the transaction of the migrations, without the lock timeout, since a concurrent build waits for the transactions that were open and keeps nobody waiting; the migration then finds the index there. An index a build left unfinished is there by its name and used by nothing: the next run drops it and builds it again. On a new database the migration builds it, over a table with nothing in it.
6. **The measurement is kept in the repository.** `eval:hub` fills a database to the shape above, from the Markdown files of directories it is given, and says for each statement how many rows of the entries and how many pages it read and through which indexes, for five questions: the commonest word, a rare one, one that is nowhere, four common words, and none. The rows and the pages are what a change is held against: a fast disk hides in milliseconds what a deployment's storage shows.

## Consequences

- What a word costs is the entries that hold it. On the database above, the count of a word that is nowhere reads 47 pages and its match 4, and at twice the size 83 and 4; a rare word the same. The commonest word, which every searchable entry holds, costs the count 3 thousand pages, and the match 80 thousand, since every entry that holds a word is ranked and its vector unpacked for that; at twice the size, twice that. A question of many common words is the slowest a search by words gets, a second and a quarter at twice the size.
- A search no longer slows with the repositories a deployment holds beside its packs. It still follows what is searchable, and the entries of commits and repositories that are not searched but hold a word are read as rows, not as vectors, before they are left out.
- The comparison by meaning starts from the searchable entries, and then costs what it did: every section vector of the packs searched, 1.7 million pages for 196 thousand vectors. It answered in 1.2 to 1.9 seconds where the vectors were in the database's memory, and in over eleven at twice the size, where they were not: the search then answers by its words alone, as ADR-0070 has it, after the time limit. Point 5 of that decision stands, and this is the measurement it waited for; what follows from it is a decision of its own.
- `migrate` takes as long as reading a large table twice when a release adds an index to it, and the versions before and after keep writing meanwhile. A run cut short is finished by the next.
- Rejected: a count kept on each snapshot when it is indexed (the indexes there are would have to be made again or counted afterwards, and the count per word needs the lookup all the same); counts that are estimates (the ranking would change with them); the plain build in the migration (it holds off the writes of a table every index is written to); a longer time limit as the answer (the cost grew with every repository opened); an approximate index of the vectors in the same change (it is not what ran out of time, and its own measurement is above).
