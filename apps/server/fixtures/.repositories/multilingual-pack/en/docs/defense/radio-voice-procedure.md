# Radio voice procedure

This publication of the Materiel Standards Directorate (MSD) is the operator's guide to speaking on the radio nets of the Vendrel Federal Defense Force (VFDF). It restates the rules of the Directorate's standard VDS 1301 for everyone who holds a handset, from a rifleman on a platoon net to the operator of a formation command net.

## The phonetic alphabet and figures

Letters are spoken with the international phonetic alphabet, Alfa, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India, Juliett, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango, Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee and Zulu, whenever a word must be spelled, a call sign is given or a letter stands alone, and a word is spelled only after the proword I SPELL. Figures are spoken digit by digit, so that 1,400 is "one four zero zero" and never "fourteen hundred", and the digits are pronounced as written in VDS 1301, with "fife" for five and "niner" for nine so that they are not confused with "fire" and "nein" on a noisy net. A decimal point is spoken as DECIMAL, and a grid reference is read as its grid zone, square and digits without pause between the easting and the northing.

## Prowords and their meaning

A proword has a fixed meaning and replaces a sentence. OVER means the transmission has ended and a reply is expected; OUT means the exchange has ended and no reply is expected; the two are never spoken together. ROGER means "I have received your last transmission" and says nothing about compliance; WILCO means "I understand and will comply" and includes ROGER, so the two are never combined. SAY AGAIN asks for a repetition of the whole transmission or the part named after ALL AFTER or ALL BEFORE; "repeat" is never used instead, having a separate meaning on fire-control nets. RADIO CHECK asks for a report of signal strength and readability. CORRECTION cancels the words just spoken and introduces the right ones. WAIT means a pause of a few seconds; WAIT OUT a longer pause after which the speaker will call back.

## The call sign format of the VFDF

A station of the VFDF is identified by a call sign of two letters and two digits, such as KV41, assigned from the formation's call sign list and spoken "Kilo Victor four one". The bare call sign belongs to the control station of the net, normally the headquarters of the unit; a subordinate station adds a letter, so that KV41A, KV41B and KV41C are the first, second and third subordinate units on the net, and a detachment or vehicle of a subordinate station adds a digit, KV41A2 being the second vehicle of the first subordinate unit. A collective call sign, formed by replacing the suffix with the word ALL, addresses every station on the net at once. Call signs change on the date the formation's communications instruction names, and a station never uses a call sign from an expired list.

## Radio checks and signal reports

A radio check is made by calling the station with the proword RADIO CHECK, and the answer reports the signal in two parts. Strength is reported as LOUD, GOOD, WEAK, VERY WEAK or FADING, and readability as CLEAR, READABLE, UNREADABLE, DISTORTED or WITH INTERFERENCE, so that a full answer is "KV41A this is KV41, LOUD AND CLEAR, over"; when both are at their best, LOUD AND CLEAR is the whole answer. A station is reported UNREADABLE only when its carrier is detected, and otherwise as NOTHING HEARD to the control station. The control station makes a radio check with every station of the net when the net opens and after every change of frequency, and a station that fails two checks in a row moves to the alternate frequency given in the communications instruction and calls the control station there.

## The date-time group

Time is given as a date-time group (DTG) of six digits, the day of the month and the 24-hour time, followed by a time zone letter, the month and the year, for example 231430Z OCT 26 for 14:30 universal time on 23 October 2026. The letter Z denotes universal time and is used on every net spanning more than one zone and on every written message; a local time carries its zone letter, so that a unit in the Federation's central zone writes 231530A for the same instant. A DTG is spoken digit by digit with the letter phonetic, "two three one four three zero Zulu". A message is identified by the DTG of its origination, not of its transmission, and two messages from one station never carry the same DTG; the second is given the next minute.

## Net discipline and transmission length

A net is a shared resource, and the control station directs it. A station listens before transmitting and breaks into an exchange only with the proword FLASH for a message of the highest precedence. Transmissions are kept short: a single transmission does not exceed 20 seconds, and a message longer than that is broken with the proword MORE TO FOLLOW and sent in parts, each acknowledged by the receiving station, so that the net is never held by one station and a jammed or stepped-on part can be repeated alone. Operators speak steadily, slightly slower than in conversation, with the handset two to three centimetres from the mouth, and never use profanity, personal names, ranks or unit names in clear. An operator who has nothing to send keeps off the net; "nothing further" is not a transmission.

## Authentication

Stations on a VFDF net may be required to prove that they are what they claim to be, and the procedure for this is authentication. Authentication is carried out with the current authentication table issued through the formation's communications instruction, and it is demanded whenever a station joins a net, whenever an order is received that changes a mission or a location, whenever a station is suspected of being an intruder and whenever the communications instruction requires it. The table in force changes on the schedule the instruction gives, and an expired table is destroyed the same day. The method of forming and answering a challenge is taught on the operator course and is not written in this publication; an operator who has not been issued the current table does not transmit anything that would require authenticating.

## The FR-23 manpack radio and its controls

The FR-23 is the manpack VHF radio of the VFDF's dismounted units, carried in the pack frame with the whip antenna or on the vehicle antenna mount. Its front panel carries the operator's controls: a channel selector with eight presets and a manual position, a volume knob with a detent at the lowest usable setting, a squelch switch with positions OFF, ON and TONE, a power switch with positions LOW, HIGH and WHISPER for use close to the enemy, and a three-bar battery indicator read at every PMCS. The handset connects to the six-pin connector on the top face; the second connector takes a remote control cable of up to 30 metres. The operator may change channel, volume, squelch and power; the presets, frequency plan and any fill are loaded by the unit's communications sergeant and not altered by the operator.

## The radio log on Form MSD-22

Every net control station, and every other station the communications instruction names, keeps a radio log on Form MSD-22 while the net is open. The log records for each transmission sent or received its DTG, the calling and called call signs, the text or a summary, and the action taken, in order of occurrence and in ink, with a wrong entry struck through once and initialled, never erased. The log also records every opening and closing of the net, every change of frequency and every radio check that was not LOUD AND CLEAR. The operator going off shift signs the log and the relieving operator signs below before taking the handset. Completed logs are handed to the unit's communications sergeant daily and kept for one year, or until an inquiry that cites them is closed.
