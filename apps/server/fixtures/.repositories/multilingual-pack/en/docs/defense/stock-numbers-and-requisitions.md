# Stock numbers and requisitions

This regulation of the Materiel Standards Directorate (MSD) governs how the Vendrel Federal Defense Force (VFDF) identifies the items it supplies and how a unit asks for them. It applies to every supply activity and to every unit that holds property, from the supply sergeant of a company to the depots of the Directorate.

## The structure of a stock number

A Vendrel stock number has thirteen digits in three groups. The first four digits are the supply class, which groups items by what they are: 2320 is trucks and truck tractors, 5820 is radio and television communication equipment, 6135 is primary batteries and 8415 is clothing. The next two digits are the bureau code, 81 for every item catalogued by the Federation, so that a Vendrel number is told apart from the numbers of other bureaus at a glance. The last seven digits are the item number, written as three digits, a hyphen and four digits, and they identify one item and nothing else: the handset of the force's radio sets is 5820-81-104-2213, and the 24 volt primary battery that powers field telephones is 6135-81-118-7720. The item number is assigned once by the cataloguing section and is never reused.

## Stock numbers, part numbers and the supplier code

A stock number names an item of supply, which may be made by several manufacturers under several part numbers. The catalogue record of a stock number lists every approved manufacturer with its part number and its Vendrel Supplier Code (VSC), a five-character code of letters and digits assigned to each supplier, such as VSC 7K41A, so that "VSC 7K41A, part 3360-114" and "VSC 2M08C, part HS-41/B" may both be the handset 5820-81-104-2213. A unit requisitions by stock number, never by part number, because a part number ties the order to one manufacturer and a stock number to any approved one. When an item has no stock number yet, the unit requests cataloguing through the supply officer with the manufacturer's part number, the VSC and a description, and the cataloguing section answers within 30 days with a number or a reason.

## The requisition on Form MSD-7

A unit asks for an item on Form MSD-7, the requisition, with one stock number per line, the quantity, the unit of issue, the priority designator, the required delivery date and the account to be charged. The requisition number is the unit's six-character activity code, the four-digit day of the year and a four-digit serial, so that a unit may raise 9,999 requisitions a day, and the number is quoted on every follow-up. The supply officer signs the form, or the unit commander when the priority is A. The receiving supply activity confirms receipt with a status card within one working day and sends a supply status whenever the expected delivery date changes. A requisition without a stock number, without a priority or without a signature is rejected with status code BA, "rejected, incomplete", and the unit raises a new one.

## Priority designators and delivery times

Every requisition carries an urgency of need designator A, B or C, which with the unit's force activity designator produces its priority. Urgency A means the unit cannot perform its mission without the item, or a deadlined item waits for it; the supply system delivers within three days inside the Federation and seven days to a deployed unit. Urgency B means the unit's capability is impaired, for example a partially mission capable item or a stock below its reorder point; delivery is within twelve days inside the Federation and twenty days deployed. Urgency C is routine replenishment and initial issue, delivered within 30 days. At most ten per cent of a unit's requisitions in a quarter may be urgency A, and the supply officer of the logistic support group checks every A requisition against the readiness report before passing it.

## Substitutes and interchangeability

When a stock number is not available, the catalogue record says what may be issued instead. An interchangeable item fits, functions and lasts as the requested item does in every application and may be issued without asking the requester; a substitute serves in some applications only, and the supply activity issues it only after the requester has agreed in writing to the substitution for the stated use. The Directorate's engineering section, not a supply activity or a manufacturer, declares interchangeability, and the declaration names the direction: an item may replace an older one without the older one replacing it. A substitute issued against an urgency A requisition is reported on the status card with status code BG, "substitute issued", and the original line remains open until the unit cancels it or the substitute is accepted.

## Shelf life codes

Items that deteriorate in storage carry a shelf life code in the catalogue record and on the package. Codes A to M give a shelf life from one month (A) to 60 months (M) for type I items, whose life cannot be extended, such as sealed primary batteries, elastomer seals and medical items; codes 1 to 9 give the life for type II items, extendable after an inspection or test described in the catalogue record, such as lubricants, paints and adhesives. The battery 6135-81-118-7720 is code G, 24 months, type I. Storage activities issue shelf life items oldest first, inspect type II items in the month their life ends and mark an extension on the package with the new date and the inspector's initials. A type I item past its date is condemned, not extended.

## Condition codes

Every item in a supply activity carries a condition code that says what may be done with it. Code A is serviceable and issuable without qualification; code B is serviceable with a qualification, such as a limited remaining shelf life or a minor defect that does not affect use; code F is unserviceable but repairable at an intermediate or depot level; code H is unserviceable and condemned, beyond economical repair or unsafe. Only a technical inspector of a maintenance activity may assign codes F and H, recording the reason on the tag; a supply clerk may assign A and B on receipt. Code F items are held apart from issuable stock and sent to repair within 30 days; code H items are held for disposal and never issued, lent or cannibalized without a written order of the Directorate.

## Turn-in of excess on Form MSD-9

Property a unit no longer needs, holds above its authorization or cannot use because it is unserviceable is turned in on Form MSD-9, the turn-in document, within 30 days of becoming excess. The form lists each item with its stock number, quantity, condition code and any serial number, and is signed by the supply officer; the receiving supply activity signs the second copy, which the unit keeps as proof that the property left its books. Serviceable excess returns to the supply system for reissue, and the unit receives credit at the catalogue price when the item is in condition A or B. The receiving activity inspects items turned in without a condition code, assigns the code and may charge the unit for the inspection. Classified, sensitive and controlled items are turned in by hand, never by common carrier.

## Inventory counts

A unit counts its property to confirm that the records match what is on the shelf. Sensitive items, including weapons, night vision devices and controlled cryptographic items, are counted monthly by serial number, and the count is signed by an officer other than the custodian. All other property is counted once a year in a wall-to-wall inventory, during which issues and receipts are suspended for the counted location, and a change of the responsible officer triggers a full inventory within 30 days whatever the date of the last one. A discrepancy is a difference between record and count that the records do not explain; a shortage above VF 500 or any shortage of a sensitive item is reported the day it is found, and the responsible officer has ten working days to find the item or begin a report of survey.
