# Test and evaluation of materiel

This regulation of the Materiel Standards Directorate (MSD) governs how materiel acquired for the Vendrel Federal Defense Force (VFDF) is tested before it is accepted. It applies to program offices, to the test establishments and to the contractors whose items are under test.

## Developmental and operational test and evaluation

Test and evaluation has two parts with different owners. Developmental test and evaluation (DT&E) asks whether the item meets its specification; it is planned by the program office, run by the Materiel Test Establishment (MTE) of the Directorate in controlled conditions, and may use instrumented prototypes and contractor personnel. Operational test and evaluation (OT&E) asks whether the item is effective and suitable in the hands of soldiers under realistic conditions; it is run by the Operational Test Group (OTG) of the force's training command, independent of the program office, with field units and production-representative items. DT&E ends before OT&E begins, except that an early operational assessment may run on prototypes to shape the design. The OTG reports to the Chief of the Defense Staff, not the Directorate, and no program enters full-rate production before the OTG report is signed.

## The test readiness review

No test phase begins without a test readiness review (TRR), chaired by the program manager for DT&E and by the OTG commander for OT&E. The review confirms that the test plan is approved, that the items under test have the configuration the plan names, that the test site, instrumentation and data reduction tools are in place and calibrated, that the safety release is signed and that the test personnel are trained on the item. The review is held between 30 and 10 days before the first test event, and it ends in one of three findings: ready, ready with actions to be closed before a named date, or not ready. A not ready finding stops the schedule, and the program manager reports the delay to the Directorate within five working days with a recovery plan.

## Climatic test methods under VDS 1210

Environmental testing of VFDF materiel follows VDS 1210, which collects the climatic methods as numbered parts. Part 1, high temperature, exposes the item in a chamber to the storage and operating temperatures of its climatic category, with the item operated at the hot limit. Part 2, low temperature, does the same at the cold limit and times the item's return to operation after a cold start. Part 3, humidity, cycles the item between warm humid and cooler conditions over several days so that condensation forms inside it, looking for corrosion, swelling and electrical leakage. Part 6, salt fog, exposes the item to an atomized salt solution for 48 hours, then dries it and examines coatings, connectors and moving parts. Each part states the number of cycles, the chamber tolerances and the functional checks before, during and after exposure.

## Dynamic test methods under VDS 1211

The dynamic methods are collected in VDS 1211. Part 1, vibration, shakes the item on an electrodynamic or hydraulic table with a spectrum derived from its platform, such as a wheeled vehicle on secondary roads or a transport aircraft, for a duration scaled from the expected service life, with the item operated during exposure where its role demands it. Part 2, shock, applies a defined pulse on a shock machine or drops the packaged item from a stated height onto each face, edge and corner, and adds a transit drop for items carried by hand. The item is examined after each axis and pulse for loosened fasteners, cracked solder joints and shifted components, and a failure suspends the remaining axes until the cause is understood. Both standards are cited by part number in every test plan.

## Reliability terms and their demonstration

Reliability requirements are written in terms the test can measure. Mean time between failures (MTBF) is the total operating time of the test population divided by the number of relevant failures, and a relevant failure is one the failure review board, chaired by the MTE, attributes to the item's design or manufacture rather than to the test, to maintenance error or to a previous failure. Mean time to repair (MTTR) is the average active corrective maintenance time, measured from the start of fault isolation to the end of the checkout, for the maintenance level the manual assigns. A reliability demonstration runs the item for a planned number of hours and accepts or rejects the MTBF against a statistical plan with stated consumer and producer risks, so that a demonstration is never judged on the point estimate alone.

## Operational availability

Operational availability (Ao) is the share of calendar time in which the item is capable of its mission, and is what the OTG reports. It is computed as uptime divided by the sum of uptime and downtime, where downtime includes active repair time and the time waiting for parts and for maintenance personnel, so that Ao reflects the supply and maintenance system as much as the design. The requirement is stated for a defined operating profile, such as 20 hours of use a day for 30 days in a deployed unit, and OT&E records every minute of downtime by cause. An Ao requirement of 0.90 or above must be shown with field maintenance and the authorized stock of spares, not with contractor support, and the OTG report separates downtime attributable to the item from that attributable to the supply system.

## The elements of integrated logistics support

An item enters service with its support in place, and the program office plans that support as integrated logistics support (ILS) from the first acquisition phase. The ILS plan covers ten elements: maintenance planning; supply support, including the initial provisioning of spares; support and test equipment; technical data, including the technical manuals; training and training devices; manpower and personnel; facilities; packaging, handling, storage and transportation; computer resources support; and design interface, through which supportability shapes the design. Each element has a named owner in the program office and a milestone in the acquisition schedule, and the ILS plan is a document under configuration control. The OTG assesses the maturity of every element during OT&E and may declare an item unsuitable for its supportability alone, whatever its performance.

## Acceptance and the deficiency report on Form MSD-31

The Directorate accepts an item when it has passed its acceptance test procedure, the inspection and test run on each production lot, and every open deficiency is dispositioned. A deficiency found anywhere from DT&E to field use is reported on Form MSD-31, the deficiency report, which names the item, its serial number and configuration, the failure conditions, the symptom, the suspected cause and the operational impact. A report is category I when the deficiency may cause death or serious injury, loss of the item or failure of a mission, and the program office answers it within 24 hours; category II covers every other deficiency and is answered within 30 days. The failure review board dispositions every report as a design change, a manufacturing correction, a procedure change or no action; no category I report closes without a verified fix.

## Configuration audits

Before production is accepted, two audits confirm that what will be built is what was tested. The functional configuration audit verifies against the test records that the item performs every function its specification requires and that every planned test was run and passed or has an approved waiver. The physical configuration audit compares a production-representative item, part by part, with the drawings, parts lists and software version description that will define the production baseline, and notes every difference between the item as built and as drawn. Both audits are chaired by the Directorate's configuration manager, attended by the MTE and the contractor, and closed with a signed minute listing the open items and their closure dates. The production baseline is established on the day the physical audit is closed, and any later change goes through an engineering change proposal.
