# Levels of maintenance and the maintenance request

This regulation of the Materiel Standards Directorate (MSD) applies to every unit of the Vendrel Federal Defense Force (VFDF) that operates, maintains or repairs materiel. It says who may do what to a piece of equipment, how its condition is reported, how work is requested and how equipment moves between levels of maintenance.

## The three levels and who performs them

The VFDF maintains its materiel at three levels. Unit maintenance is performed by the operator or crew and by the unit's own maintenance section, under the unit commander, with the tools and spares the unit carries. Intermediate maintenance is performed by the maintenance companies of the logistic support groups, which serve the units in their area and hold the test equipment and the authorized stock of repairable assemblies. Depot maintenance is performed by the two depots of the Directorate and by contractors under its supervision, and it alone may overhaul, rebuild or modify an item. The level is a matter of authorization, not of location: a depot team working forward still performs depot maintenance, and a unit mechanic in a depot still performs unit maintenance. The maintenance allocation chart in each item's manuals assigns every task to a level.

## What each level may do

Unit maintenance covers inspection, lubrication, adjustment, cleaning, the replacement of parts reachable without special tools, such as lamps, filters, belts and batteries, and the replacement of line-replaceable units when the manual allots the task. Intermediate maintenance adds the repair of assemblies by replacing their components, the use of test and diagnostic equipment, welding and machining of a limited kind, calibration of unit test equipment, and the holding of a repairable exchange float from which a unit receives a serviceable assembly against an unserviceable one. Depot maintenance covers overhaul to a like-new standard, rebuild, the application of modifications, and the manufacture of parts no longer supplied. A level never performs a task allotted to a higher one, even with the tools at hand, except under a written waiver of the Directorate for one item and one occasion.

## PMCS and its intervals

Preventive maintenance checks and services (PMCS) are the inspections and services an operator performs at fixed intervals to find faults before they stop the equipment. The intervals are before operation, during operation, after operation, weekly, monthly, quarterly, semiannual and annual, and each item's operator manual lists the checks of each interval in a table with the item number, the item to check, the procedure and the condition that makes the equipment not fully mission capable. Before-operation checks are performed on every day the equipment is used and after-operation checks on every day it has been used; longer checks fall within a window of ten per cent of the interval. The operator records faults on the equipment inspection and maintenance worksheet, which the unit maintenance supervisor reviews every working day, raising a maintenance request for any fault the operator cannot correct.

## Not-mission-capable criteria

Equipment is reported in one of three readiness conditions. It is fully mission capable when it can perform all its missions; partially mission capable when it can perform some but not all, for example a truck with a failed winch; and not mission capable, also called deadlined, when it can perform none. The deadline criteria of an item are the faults listed in the "not fully mission capable if" column of its PMCS table, and such a fault deadlines the item whatever the operator thinks of its seriousness. Brakes, steering, night lights, seat belts and fire extinguishers are deadline items on every vehicle of the force. A deadlined item is reported in the day's readiness report, is operated only to move it to a repair location, and carries a red tag on the operator's controls until the fault is cleared.

## The maintenance request on Form MSD-14 and its priorities

Work beyond the operator's authority is requested on Form MSD-14, the maintenance request, which names the unit, the item and its serial number, the fault as observed, the hours or kilometres on the item, the readiness condition and the priority. Priority 1 is for a deadlined item of a unit on operations or in the 30-day deployment window; work begins within 24 hours of receipt. Priority 2 is for a deadlined item of any other unit or a partially mission capable item of a deploying unit; work begins within five working days. Priority 3 is for every other fault, and work begins within 20 working days. The receiving maintenance activity acknowledges the form within one working day with a job order number, and a priority may be raised only by the requesting unit's commander in writing.

## Evacuation to a higher level

An item the receiving level cannot repair within its authorization, or within 30 days for want of a part, is evacuated to the next level with the Form MSD-14 that opened the job, the item's maintenance record and a statement of the work already done. The evacuating activity packs the item, removes fuel, ammunition and classified components and raises the transport request; the receiving activity inspects the item within three working days and accepts it or returns it with a written reason. Components removed before evacuation are listed on the form and travel with the item or stay with the evacuating unit, never used elsewhere. An item evacuated to depot leaves the unit's readiness report and is replaced, where a float exists, by a serviceable item of the same type, returned when the unit's own item comes back.

## Technical manual numbering

Every end item is supported by a series of technical manuals (TM) numbered after the seven-digit item number of its stock number, followed by a level suffix. For the LT-6 light truck, stock number 2320-81-131-0045, the operator manual is TM 131-0045-10, the unit maintenance manual TM 131-0045-20 and the intermediate maintenance manual TM 131-0045-30; the depot manual carries the suffix -40 and the repair parts and special tools list the suffix -P, so TM 131-0045-20P lists the parts a unit may order. A -10 manual holds the operating instructions, the PMCS table and the troubleshooting an operator may attempt; a -20 manual holds the maintenance allocation chart and the unit tasks; a -30 manual holds the intermediate tasks and the test procedures. A manual change is issued as a numbered change page set and the title page records the changes posted.

## Controlled exchange

Controlled exchange is the removal of a serviceable part from an unserviceable item to make another item of the same type serviceable. It is permitted at unit and intermediate level only when the part is not available in the supply system within the time the priority of the job allows, when the donor item is already not mission capable for another reason, and when the maintenance officer approves each exchange in writing on the job order. It is forbidden on items awaiting evacuation to depot, on safety-of-flight parts of aircraft, on items under warranty, and when the donor would become unrepairable at its current level. Every part removed is recorded on the donor's maintenance record and a replacement is requisitioned for the donor the same day, so that controlled exchange never becomes a way of keeping two items half serviceable.

## Maintenance records and their retention

Each end item carries a maintenance record that stays with it for life and records every PMCS fault, maintenance request, repair, modification and transfer, following VDS 1104, the Directorate's standard for maintenance records. Unit maintenance activities keep the equipment inspection and maintenance worksheets for 90 days after the faults on them are closed and the completed Form MSD-14 for two years. Intermediate and depot activities keep job orders for three years after closure and test and calibration certificates for the life of the item plus one year. The maintenance record of an item that is condemned goes to the Directorate's records section with the condemnation report and is kept for five years. Records are kept on paper or in the maintenance management system, never both for one item, and a transfer of the item moves the record with it.
