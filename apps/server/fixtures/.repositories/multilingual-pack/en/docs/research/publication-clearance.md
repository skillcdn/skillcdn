# Publication clearance and open-access deposit

This procedure governs every manuscript, abstract, poster, thesis, data set and talk that presents work of the Marrowfield Materials Characterization Centre (MMCC) to anyone outside the Centre. It applies to staff, students and visiting researchers alike, and to work done with partners when the Centre's name or data appears.

## What needs clearance and who requests it

Clearance is required before any work of the Centre is submitted, uploaded, presented or spoken about outside it, including a journal manuscript, a preprint, an abstract, a poster, an invited talk, a data set, a thesis and a press interview. The corresponding author, or the presenter when there is no written text, requests the review on Form MMCC-21, attaching the full text and figures as they will be submitted, the list of authors with affiliations, the target venue and the data tier of every data set the work draws on. The Publications Office assigns a review number of the form PR, the two-digit year and a three-digit sequence, for example PR-26-041, quoted in every later exchange about the work. A submission made without a review number is withdrawn by the Director whatever its stage at the venue.

## The three stages of the review and their deadlines

A review has three stages, each with a deadline in working days from the day the Publications Office confirms the request complete. In the scientific stage, five days, the head of the laboratory concerned confirms that the results match the laboratory notebooks and the methods are described correctly. In the compliance stage, ten days, the Data Steward checks the data tiers and the release board decisions, the Compliance Officer checks export controls and the Technology Transfer Officer decides whether a patent hold applies. In the approval stage, three days, the Director or Deputy Director signs the clearance, possibly with conditions. A manuscript built only on Tier 0 and Tier 1 data normally clears in twelve working days; one drawing on Tier 2 or Tier 3 data takes the full eighteen and may wait for a partner's consent, which stops the clock.

## Conference abstracts and posters

Abstracts and posters follow a shortened path because of their deadlines. An abstract of up to 500 words citing only Tier 0 and Tier 1 data is reviewed by the head of the laboratory and the Publications Office in three working days, and the author may submit it on a provisional clearance when the conference deadline falls earlier, provided the full review ends before the conference. A poster is reviewed as a manuscript and carries the review number in its lower left corner in at least 10 point type. An abstract mentioning a partner, a contract or an instrument acquired under a controlled program is never provisional. Slides of a talk are cleared once per subject; the speaker may reuse them for one year, and a new review is needed when a result, a figure or a partner's name is added.

## The patent hold

When the Technology Transfer Officer judges a result patentable, a patent hold is placed on the review and the work may not be disclosed in any form, including an abstract, until the hold is lifted. The hold lasts 60 days from the day it is placed, while the Officer decides with the inventors whether to file a priority application, and may be extended once by 30 days when a prior-art search is still running. A hold ends when a priority application is filed, when the Officer declines to file, or when the period expires, whichever comes first, and the author is informed in writing the same day. A hold placed during the compliance stage stops the clock. The Centre places no second hold on the same result, so an author whose hold has ended need not wait for the grant.

## Deposit in the Marrowfield Open Repository

Every cleared publication is deposited in the Marrowfield Open Repository (MOR), the Centre's open-access repository, within 30 days of acceptance. The author deposits the accepted manuscript, the published version where the licence permits and the data set the data availability statement names, and the Publications Office assigns a handle of the form MOR, the two-digit year and a four-digit sequence, for example MOR-26-0188, cited in the data availability statement of later work. Where the publisher imposes an embargo, the deposit is made at once and the file opens to the public when the embargo ends, at most twelve months after publication. A deposit may hold only Tier 0 data; a work resting on higher tiers deposits the manuscript and a description of the data with the conditions of access. The repository keeps every deposited version and never deletes one.

## Authorship and the acknowledgement of the Office

The authors of a work are those who made a substantial intellectual contribution to its conception, measurements or interpretation and approved the final text; providing an instrument, a sample or funding alone does not make an author. The corresponding author lists each author's contribution in the request, and the Director settles a dispute after hearing the parties, before the review proceeds. Every work of the Centre carries the acknowledgement "This work was carried out at the Marrowfield Materials Characterization Centre of the Office of Research and Defense Materiel of the Vendrel Federation" in the acknowledgements section, with the project number, and work funded by a formation of the VFDF names the formation and the contract number unless the contract forbids it. Technical staff who prepared samples or ran instruments are named in the acknowledgements when they are not authors.

## Data availability statements

Each manuscript carries a data availability statement saying where the data behind every figure and table is and under which conditions. For Tier 0 data the statement cites the MOR handle of the deposited data set. For Tier 1 data the statement says the data is available from the corresponding author on reasonable request, and the author must then keep the data in the project share for the retention period and answer requests within 30 days. For Tier 2 data the statement names the partner whose consent is needed and says the data is available with that consent; for Tier 3 data it says the data is subject to Federation controls and not available. A statement promising more than the tier permits is corrected in the compliance stage, and the Publications Office keeps the wording of the four standard statements.

## Theses of students

A thesis supervised at the Centre is reviewed before it goes to the examiners, because a thesis deposited in a university library is a publication. The supervisor requests the review at least 40 working days before the university's submission deadline, and the review follows the full three stages. A chapter resting on Tier 2 or Tier 3 data is written so that it can be withheld: it is bound separately, the university is asked for a restriction of access of up to five years, and the examination copy carries the restriction on the title page. The student's defense is open, but the slides of a restricted chapter are cleared separately and the restricted results are presented only in a closed session. A student may not deposit a preprint of a thesis chapter before the thesis itself has cleared.

## What is kept of a review and for how long

The Publications Office keeps for every review number the request, the submitted text as reviewed, the written opinions of each stage, the conditions of clearance, the signed clearance and, after publication, the citation and the MOR handle. The record is kept for ten years after publication or, when the work is never published, ten years after the clearance was signed; a review that carried a patent hold is kept for the life of the patent plus five years. Reviews of theses are kept for as long as the university keeps the thesis. The record is Tier 1 unless the reviewed work is of a higher tier, in which case the record takes that tier. Authors may read their own review record at any time, and the Director may release a record to a journal that investigates a disputed publication.
