# The electron microscopy facility

These rules apply to every user of the electron microscopy facility (EMF) of the Marrowfield Materials Characterization Centre (MMCC), whether staff, student or external user. They cover the instruments, their booking, the preparation of samples, the techniques available, the accounting of beam time and the conduct expected around high vacuum.

## The instruments of the facility

The facility operates three column instruments. The Halden SE-700 is a field-emission scanning electron microscope with a Schottky source, an accelerating voltage of 0.2 to 30 kV, a chamber accepting samples up to 150 mm across and a resolution of 1.0 nm at 15 kV on the gold-on-carbon test specimen. The Corvane T-300 is a 300 kV transmission electron microscope with a double-tilt goniometer of plus or minus 35 degrees in alpha and a point resolution of 0.19 nm in TEM mode. The Rilke FB-120 is a dual-beam focused ion beam instrument with a gallium liquid-metal ion source running from 2 to 30 kV and a maximum ion current of 65 nA for coarse milling, used for cross sections and lamella lift-out. Each instrument has one named instrument scientist, the only person allowed to change the stored alignments.

## Booking and the three user levels

Beam time is booked in the facility calendar in one-hour blocks, at most ten working days ahead, and a block not started within 20 minutes is released to the waiting list. A trainee has completed the safety briefing and books only sessions with an instrument scientist or a superuser at the console; after 30 logged hours on an instrument and a practical check, the instrument scientist promotes the trainee to independent user. An independent user books unaccompanied daytime sessions, from 08:00 to 18:00 on working days, and holds at most 12 hours of future bookings on one instrument. A superuser, appointed by the Facility Manager after at least 200 hours and a second check, may book nights and weekends, train others and perform the daily checks. A level is withdrawn after twelve months without a logged session.

## Sample preparation: coating, milling and sectioning

The preparation room offers three routes. Non-conducting samples for the SEM are sputter-coated with gold-palladium or carbon to a nominal 5 to 10 nm under procedure EMF-SOP-04, and the coating is noted on the sample record because it hides surface features finer than its grain. TEM foils of metals and ceramics are thinned by electropolishing or by ion milling at 3 to 5 kV with a final low-energy step at 0.5 kV to remove the amorphized layer, and a foil is accepted when an electron-transparent area of at least 20 micrometres across exists. Polymers and biological materials are sectioned on the ultramicrotome with a diamond knife to 70 to 90 nm and collected on carbon-filmed grids. Preparation tools are cleaned by the user after every session, and a diamond knife that has touched a metal is withdrawn for inspection.

## Detectors and techniques: EDS, EBSD and STEM

The SEM carries a silicon drift energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometer (EDS) with a 60 mm2 active area, used for compositional maps and point analyses of elements from boron upwards, with the understanding that elements below one weight per cent are reported as detected rather than quantified. The same instrument has an electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) camera for grain orientation maps; EBSD requires a vibration-polished or ion-polished surface and a 70 degree tilt, and a map is accepted when the indexing rate exceeds 85 per cent. The TEM offers scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM) with bright-field and high-angle annular dark-field detectors, an EDS detector for nanoscale composition and selected-area diffraction with camera lengths from 80 to 2000 mm. Every quantified result carries the standard used and the date of the last detector calibration, which the instrument scientist performs monthly.

## Cryo holders and vacuum transfer

Samples sensitive to air or to beam heating are handled cold. The TEM has a single-tilt cryo holder cooled with liquid nitrogen that reaches 95 K at the specimen after 40 minutes and is pumped in the holder station for at least two hours before each use; the SEM has a cold stage reaching 140 K for frozen hydrated specimens. Air-sensitive samples, such as lithium-containing electrode materials and alkali halides, move from the glove box to the instrument in the vacuum transfer capsule under procedure EMF-SOP-11, which keeps them below 10 pascals and records the capsule pressure at loading and docking. A cryo session is booked as a four-hour minimum and the holder is returned to the station, warmed and pumped by the user before the next session, since a holder left wet at room temperature takes a day to recover.

## Beam time accounting

Every session is charged to a project from console login to logout, in beam hours rounded up to the next quarter hour, and the facility calendar is the only record that counts. Internal projects are charged the internal rate set each year by the Director; external academic users pay the full-cost rate and industrial users the commercial rate, both published on the facility page in Vendrel florins per hour. A session cancelled less than 24 hours ahead is charged at half, and a session lost to an instrument fault is not charged, which the instrument scientist confirms in the log. The Facility Manager issues a statement of beam hours to every project leader at the end of each month, and a dispute must be raised within ten working days, after which the charge stands.

## Data export from the instrument computers

Instrument computers are not on the Centre's network, so data leaves them only through the facility's export station. The user copies the session folder, named with the date and project number, to the export station over the facility's isolated link, where the files are scanned and moved to the project share by the export service within one hour; nothing is attached to an instrument computer directly, and a user who inserts a storage device into a console loses independent status for three months. Images are kept in the native format with their metadata and as TIFF, spectra and EBSD maps in the vendor's format with a text export. Instrument computers delete a session folder after 14 days, so a user who has not exported by then must repeat the measurement. Data of controlled projects is exported by the instrument scientist alone.

## Vacuum incidents

A vacuum incident is any unplanned loss of column or chamber vacuum, a specimen or holder falling in the chamber, a cryo holder warming during a session or a liquid-nitrogen spill into an instrument. The user stops the beam, closes the column valve where there is one, leaves the instrument as it is and calls the instrument scientist, the only person allowed to vent, open or restart it; a user never tries to recover a dropped specimen. The incident is written up on Form MMCC-17 within one working day, with the pressure readings, the holder and specimen involved and the sequence of events, and the instrument returns to service only after the instrument scientist has verified the vacuum against the daily-check baseline. A user involved in two incidents in a year repeats the training at the trainee level.

## Contamination and column hygiene

Hydrocarbon contamination is the facility's most common cause of degraded images, and users are responsible for keeping it out. Samples are handled only with clean tweezers, never with gloves that have touched anything else; samples that outgas, such as fresh resins, uncured adhesives or oily machined parts, are baked or plasma-cleaned before loading, and a 5-minute plasma cleaner run before any high-resolution TEM session is compulsory. A user who observes a dark contamination square growing under a stationary beam reports it in the log, since a rising rate tells the instrument scientist that the column needs attention. The TEM column is baked by the instrument scientist every quarter and the SEM chamber is wiped and the stage lubricated at the same interval, during a maintenance week that is announced in the calendar two weeks ahead and blocks all bookings.
